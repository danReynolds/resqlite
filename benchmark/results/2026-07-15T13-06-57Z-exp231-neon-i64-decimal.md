# Exp 231 — NEON i64→decimal kernel, focused A/B

Harness: `benchmark/experiments/select_bytes_int_heavy.dart`
Device: Apple Silicon (arm64), macOS. **Shared dev machine under heavy
concurrent load** (15+ experiment worktrees active) — the identical-code
control lanes drift ±10–120% between paired runs, so only the *direction and
sign* of the target lane, held across every methodology, is load-bearing. No
single pair is clean enough to quote a precise magnitude.

Baseline = `origin/main` (scalar two-digit-table itoa, exp 192).
Candidate = NEON i64→decimal kernel for magnitudes ≥ 1e8 (archived at
`archive/exp-231`).

All medians in µs/query (lower = faster). The `x 20 small non-neg (0..9999)`
and `x 8 small non-neg` lanes run **identical scalar code** in both builds — they
are pure noise gauges, not measurements of the change.

## Read

Across 11 passes in three methodologies the candidate is **never** convincingly
faster on the big-int target: +1.2/+6.0/+9.8% (baseline-first), +28/+36/+16%
(candidate-first), +11.3% at the least-contaminated min-of-N, and ~flat in the
one quiet solo pair. A real ≥5% kernel win would surface as candidate-faster in
at least the quieter passes; it never does, while the identical-code controls
scatter symmetrically around zero. Verdict: no end-to-end win — a small
regression, consistent with paying an out-of-line call + vector setup per single
integer with no cross-cell batching to amortize it. Rejected.
