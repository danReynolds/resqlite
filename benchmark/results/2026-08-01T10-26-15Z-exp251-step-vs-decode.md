# Experiment 251: SQLite step vs Dart result construction

Collected 2026-08-01 on arm64 macOS 26.2 with Dart 3.12.2. The benchmark was
built as a native-asset-aware AOT CLI bundle from current `origin/main` plus
the measurement harness:

```console
dart build cli \
  --target=benchmark/experiments/step_vs_decode.dart \
  --output=/tmp/resqlite-exp251-direct-v2
/tmp/resqlite-exp251-direct-v2/bundle/bin/step_vs_decode
```

Each repeat is a fresh process. Within a process, the direct-handle lanes run
interleaved with a rotating first lane (15 rounds, 6 queries per sample); the
real `Database.select()` denominator uses 9 samples of 15 queries. Values below
are the per-process medians in microseconds.

`step` acquires and steps the real cached statement through
`resqliteStepRow`, materializing no Dart cell values and performing no synthetic
per-cell Dart work. `full` uses the production `executeQuery` worker path.
`full - step` is therefore an estimate of Dart decode/result construction,
not a sampled CPU profile. `bytes` is the whole `executeQueryBytes` path and is
shown only as a different-representation reference; it uses a distinct native
query/JSON path, so `bytes - step` is not interpreted as pure encoding cost.
`select` is the same SQL through the public reader pool, observed on main.

## Across-process medians

Component values are medians of the five per-process medians. Percentages are
medians of the five paired per-process ratios rather than ratios of marginal
medians.

| shape | step | full | decode/result build | select | step/full | build/full | full/select | build/select |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 10k x 20 INTEGER | 2600.2 | 5123.8 | 2524.5 | 5610.7 | 50.7% | 49.3% | 91.2% | 45.0% |
| 10k x 4 INTEGER + 4 TEXT | 1331.2 | 3587.3 | 2259.8 | 3889.6 | 37.0% | 63.0% | 92.0% | 57.9% |
| 5k x 4 INTEGER | 320.0 | 533.3 | 212.5 | 632.9 | 60.2% | 39.8% | 84.0% | 33.5% |
| 5k x 1 TEXT | 222.8 | 458.8 | 233.2 | 483.7 | 48.8% | 51.2% | 94.3% | 48.2% |

The direct worker-path `full` proxy is typically 84-94% of separately measured
main-observed `select()` latency, but its internals expose no universal dominant
target. The mixed wide row is result-construction-heavy (63%), the small
integer row is step-heavy (60%), and the other two are roughly even. The
estimated result-construction share of observed latency ranges from 34% to 58%.

`executeQueryBytes` is 10-40% faster than `executeQuery` on these same SQL
shapes, but it returns different semantics and representation; this is evidence
for native-byte consumers, not permission to replace `ResultSet` or to claim
main-isolate CPU savings.

An earlier `dart compile exe` attempt was discarded before measurement because
that standalone executable did not bundle the package native asset. Every
number above comes from the valid `dart build cli` bundle. A preflight version
of the harness that folded two cell words in Dart was also discarded; the
published rerun removes that synthetic work from the step reference.
